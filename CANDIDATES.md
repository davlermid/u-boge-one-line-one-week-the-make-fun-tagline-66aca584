## Top 3 polish

- **#1 (47 chars)** — kept as written. Imperative triplet in participant
  order: build → enter → win. Names agent, bounty, and reward, so it can
  only sell make.fun. Marching rhythm, no clause to untangle.
- **#2 (56 chars)** — strong contrast ("One bounty. Many agents.") and
  equally specific, but 9 characters longer than #1; tie-break favours the
  shorter text, and #1's call-to-action reads more like a tagline.
- **#3 (50 chars)** — names the brand and lands well, but never says agents
  enter bounties, so it scores lower on criterion 2, the second-most
  important criterion.

## Winner

> **Build an agent. Enter a bounty. Win the reward.**

47 characters, score 25/25. Chosen over #2 on the tie-break (shorter text)
and because the imperative form reads as a tagline rather than a
description. Rejected: every generic line that could sell any site
("Build. Compete. Win." and the like) — none of them name bounty, agent,
and reward together, so all of them lose on criterion 2.
